# Pilot – How long would it take you to reach the nearest star?

**Format:** narrated long-form, ~10 min (≈1,500 words at 150 wpm), scale animations in the By-The-Numbers engine, own music (cinematic/synthwave), CC0 SFX.
**Hook (hookscore.py):** 82 / 100 STRONG – formula *The Question* + timely *Deadline* angle (Voyager 1 reaches one light-day from Earth on 18 Nov 2026).
**Runtime check:** count words after the final edit; target 1,400–1,600.

Travel times = 40.208 × 10¹² km ÷ speed ÷ 8,766 h per year (non-stop, straight line, no acceleration).
Walking 5 km/h → 917 million years · car 100 km/h → 46 million · jet 900 km/h → 5.1 million · Voyager 1 (61,200 km/h) → 75,000 · Parker record (692,000 km/h) → 6,600 · light → 4.24 years.

## Script

### 0:00 – HOOK
> Voyager 1 is about to be one light-day from you. How long would you need to reach the nearest star… before you die?

[ON SCREEN: black → Voyager 1 drifting, a counter ticks "23 h 59 min 58 s … 1 LIGHT-DAY". Hard cut to a tiny glowing dot: "PROXIMA CENTAURI · 1,549 light-days".]

### 0:12 – THE TURN (say what we do, then do it)
> Today we race five ways to get there – on foot, by car, by jet, with the fastest machine we've ever built, and at the speed of light. And we'll find the exact moment where a human life runs out.

[ON SCREEN: five lanes appear with icons and labels: WALK · CAR · JET · PARKER PROBE · LIGHT. A small human silhouette at the start line. Progress bar 1/5 top.]

### 0:35 – BEAT 1 · What "4.24 light-years" really means
> The nearest star is Proxima Centauri. It's 4.24 light-years away. That sounds close. It's forty trillion kilometers.
> Light reaches the Moon in just over one second. It reaches the Sun in eight minutes and twenty seconds. Voyager 1 – launched in 1977, the farthest thing humans have ever built – needed almost half a century to get one light-day away.
> Proxima is one thousand, five hundred and forty-nine light-days away.

[ON SCREEN: scale-zoom (bn2 engine): Earth → Moon (1.3 light-seconds) → Sun (8 min 20 s) → Voyager 1 (1 light-day, "49 years of travel") → camera keeps pulling back until Voyager is a pixel; Proxima appears at the far edge. Counter: 40,208,000,000,000 km.]

### 2:00 – BEAT 2 · On foot
> Let's start walking. Five kilometers an hour, no breaks, no sleep, a straight line.
> You'd reach the Moon in about nine years. Proxima Centauri? Nine hundred and seventeen million years.
> For scale: complex animal life exploded on Earth about five hundred and thirty-eight million years ago. You'd have had to start walking long before the first animal had eyes.

[ON SCREEN: lane 1 – walker icon, years counter racing up to 917,000,000; a geological timeline slides underneath with markers "Cambrian explosion · 538 Myr". Progress 1/5.]

### 3:15 – BEAT 3 · By car
> Upgrade to a car. A hundred kilometers an hour, forever.
> The Moon is now a road trip of a hundred and sixty days. The nearest star: forty-six million years.
> The last non-bird dinosaurs died sixty-six million years ago. If one had started driving that day, it would still have twenty million years to go.

[ON SCREEN: lane 2 – car icon; Moon checkpoint "160 days" pops; counter to 46,000,000; dinosaur silhouette with "66 Myr ago" on the timeline. Progress 2/5.]

### 4:25 – BEAT 4 · By jet
> A passenger jet at nine hundred kilometers an hour. Five point one million years.
> Our whole genus – Homo – has existed for about two point eight million years. Not even all of human evolution would get you halfway.

[ON SCREEN: lane 3 – jet; counter 5,100,000; timeline marker "Genus Homo · 2.8 Myr" sitting at the 55 % mark of the bar. Progress 3/5.]

### 5:30 – BEAT 5 · Voyager 1, the real thing
> Now the machine that actually left. Voyager 1 moves at about seventeen kilometers per second – sixty-one thousand kilometers an hour.
> If it were pointed at Proxima – it isn't – it would arrive in about seventy-five thousand years. Written human history is about five thousand years old. Fifteen times that.

[ON SCREEN: Voyager model on lane 4 (provisional), counter 75,000; history ribbon "5,000 years" copied 15 times. Small disclaimer "Voyager 1 is not heading to Proxima".]

### 6:40 – BEAT 6 · The fastest object ever built
> On Christmas Eve 2024, NASA's Parker Solar Probe hit one hundred and ninety-two kilometers per second while diving past the Sun. That's six hundred ninety-two thousand kilometers an hour – New York to Tokyo in under a minute.
> At that speed, Proxima is six thousand six hundred years away. Longer than the pyramids have existed.
> And here's the catch: Parker only reaches that speed for a moment, falling toward the Sun. We have no way to keep it.

[ON SCREEN: Parker icon with speed flare at perihelion, counter 6,600; "brief peak – not a cruising speed" tag; pyramid icon "~4,500 years" on the timeline. Progress 5/5 bar fills; riser starts.]

### 7:55 – BEAT 7 · Light
> And light? Four point two four years. A kid who watches the launch would be in school when it arrives.
> The gap between our fastest machine and light is the whole problem. It isn't distance. It's energy.

[ON SCREEN: DROP – light beam crosses all five lanes in 1 second, counter 4.24 years. All five lanes shown as a final scoreboard, log-scale bars (bn1 engine).]

### 8:40 – PAYOFF (answer the hook, explicitly)
> So – how long would you need? With anything humans have ever built: longer than human civilization. You would not make it. Neither would your great-great-great-grandchildren.
> The only serious plan on the table is Breakthrough Starshot: probes the size of a stamp, pushed by lasers to about a fifth of light speed. About twenty years. Proposed – not built.

[ON SCREEN: scoreboard with the human silhouette "YOUR LIFETIME ~80 years" line drawn across – only LIGHT and STARSHOT (dashed, "proposal") cross it.]

### 9:30 – CLOSE (one ask)
> Next week: how far Voyager 1 has really gone – and what it will hit first. Which of these five would you have bet on? Tell me in the comments.

[ON SCREEN: end screen with next-video slot + subscribe element (YouTube end-screen, 20 s).]
